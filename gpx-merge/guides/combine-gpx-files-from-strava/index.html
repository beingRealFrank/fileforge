Combine GPX files from Strava into one track

You recorded one outing as two or three activities (a pause that became a new activity, a phone and a watch, a restart after a crash) and now want a single GPX file. You can merge them on your own computer, in time order, without uploading the tracks anywhere.

Verified sample The sample below was produced by running this site's merge on two small synthetic GPX files that overlap by a few points.

Steps

  1. Download each activity as GPX. On Strava's website, open the activity, use the actions menu and choose the GPX export option for activities you recorded yourself (the exact menu position changes from time to time). Keep every part in one folder.
  2. Open the GPX, FIT and TCX merge tool and drop all the files on it. They are read in your browser and are not uploaded.
  3. Read the counts. The page shows points in, points out and how many exact duplicates were removed. If the parts overlap, the overlap is counted once.
  4. Choose segments or one continuous line. Tick the segments option to keep each file as its own segment (a clean break where you paused), or leave it off for one continuous line.
  5. Download the merged GPX and use it wherever you need the whole outing: a map, a trip report, or an upload to another service.

2 files · 13 points in · 11 points out · 2 exact duplicates removed · 1 segment(s)

What to know

  • Points are joined by their timestamps. If a file has no timestamps, files are joined in the order you added them, and the page says so.
  • GPX keeps position, time, elevation and heart rate. Power, cadence and lap data are not carried over; keep the original files if you need them.
  • Merging does not change what Strava shows for the original activities.
